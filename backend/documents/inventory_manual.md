# Inventory Management & Procurement Manual

## Inventory Management Overview

Inventory management is the process of tracking products, stock levels, purchase orders, suppliers, sales transactions, returns, transfers, and inventory adjustments. The objective is to maintain product availability while minimizing carrying costs and preventing stockouts.

Inventory records must remain accurate at all times. Every inventory movement must be recorded in the system.

---

## SKU Management

SKU stands for Stock Keeping Unit.

SKU formats are used to uniquely identify products.

Examples:

GRO-1001
GRO-1002
GRO-1003

The prefix GRO indicates grocery products.

Examples:

ELE-1001
ELE-1002

The prefix ELE indicates electronics products.

Grocery products typically use the GRO prefix while electronic products use the ELE prefix.

Each SKU must be unique.

## FIFO

FIFO means First In First Out.

Products received first should be sold first.

FIFO is especially important for grocery and perishable products.

Benefits:

- Reduced spoilage
- Lower wastage
- Better stock rotation

## Stockout

A stockout occurs when inventory reaches zero and customer demand cannot be fulfilled.

Stockouts may cause:

- Lost sales
- Customer dissatisfaction
- Emergency procurement costs

Organizations should maintain safety stock to prevent stockouts.

---

## Safety Stock

Safety stock is extra inventory maintained to absorb demand variability and supplier delays.

Safety stock reduces the risk of stockouts.

Organizations calculate safety stock based on historical demand and lead time variability.

---

## Lead Time

Lead time is the period between placing a purchase order and receiving inventory.

For example:

Purchase Order Date: Day 1

Goods Receipt Date: Day 5

Lead Time = 4 Days

Lead time is a critical component of reorder point calculations.

---

## Reorder Point

A reorder point indicates the inventory level at which replenishment should begin.

Formula:

Reorder Point = Daily Demand × Lead Time + Safety Stock

Example:

Daily Demand = 100 units

Lead Time = 5 days

Safety Stock = 200 units

Reorder Point = 100 × 5 + 200

Reorder Point = 700 units

Organizations should initiate procurement when stock reaches the reorder point.

---

## Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)

EOQ stands for Economic Order Quantity.

EOQ identifies the optimal purchase quantity that minimizes:

- Ordering costs
- Holding costs

EOQ helps organizations balance inventory investment and replenishment frequency.

## Purchase Order Approval

Question: When does a PO need Store Manager approval?

Answer:

A Purchase Order (PO) requires Store Manager approval when the PO value is ₹50,000 or more.

Approval Rules:

PO Value below ₹50,000:
Department Supervisor Approval

PO Value ₹50,000 or above:
Store Manager Approval Required

Examples:

₹25,000 -> Supervisor Approval

₹49,999 -> Supervisor Approval

₹50,000 -> Store Manager Approval

₹75,000 -> Store Manager Approval

₹100,000 -> Store Manager Approval
